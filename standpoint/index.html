Layers

    Any ArcGIS layer works: scene, feature, map image, tile, vector tile, imagery, WMS or WMTS service, or a web scene item. Elevation layers become the ground. Tiled layers must be cut in Web Mercator (EPSG:3857) to appear in this 3D scene. Non-public items ask you to sign in. Click a layer's name in the list to rename it.

    How Standpoint works

    Standpoint finds where a photograph was taken from and how the camera was pointed. You match features in the photo with the same features on the 3D mesh, and it solves the camera position, viewing direction and lens from those pairs. Hover over any control for a short explanation.

    Try the exampleLoads a sample photo with its point pairs, so you see a solved camera right away.

    The workflow

    1. Open a photo. Drop it on the photo panel, paste it, or use Open photo in the photo panel's bar.
    2. Pair points. Click a sharp feature in the photo, then the same feature on the mesh, or the other way round. The panel waiting for your click gets an amber outline and a prompt at the bottom.
    3. Read the camera. From 5 pairs the camera is solved automatically and updates with every change. More well-spread pairs make it more precise and let you catch mistakes.

    Photo panel

    • Click to add a point. Drag a marker to adjust it.
    • Drag the empty photo to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Fit shows the whole photo.
    • Magnifier shows an enlarged view under the cursor for exact placing.
    • paired point · waiting for its scene position · suspect · switched off.
    • Once solved, a cyan line runs from each marker to where the solved camera puts its 3D point. A dashed cyan ring shows where a point placed only in the scene should be in the photo.

    Scene panel

    • Click on the mesh to place a point. Clicks outside the mesh are refused.
    • Drag to pan. Right-drag to orbit around the view's center.
    • On a trackpad, orbit with a two-finger press and drag, or use the keys A D to rotate and W S to tilt. On a tablet, twist or drag two fingers.
    • Scroll to zoom in steps, or hold the middle mouse button and drag up or down to zoom smoothly toward the cursor.
    • Hold B and drag to look around from the camera's own position. Shift with the scroll wheel or middle-drag changes the field of view.
    • The Fit button in the scene bar zooms to all points in use.
    • Below the compass, the down arrow looks straight down at the spot in the middle of the view. The Look around button switches between the normal navigation and looking around, where dragging turns the camera in place, like holding B. Click it again or press Esc to go back.
    • Click a marker to select its pair.
    • Dim hidden points draws points the mesh covers from your viewpoint at half strength.
    • Once solved, the cyan pyramid shows the camera position and what it saw. Its thick edge is the top of the photo.

    Layers

    • The Prague 3D mesh is built in. Layers in the scene panel's bar lets you hide it and add others.
    • Add any ArcGIS layer by URL or item ID, with an optional name. Click a name in the list to rename it.
    • Elevation layers become the ground, so clicks outside the mesh get heights.
    • The scene is in Web Mercator (EPSG:3857). Tiled layers must be cut in EPSG:3857 to appear. The list flags any that are not.

    Point pairs

    • Residual is how far, in photo pixels, the solved camera misses that pair. A red value means the pair fits much worse than the rest; it may be matched to the wrong feature.
    • Use leaves a pair out of the solution without deleting it.
    • Row buttons: place again in the photo, place again in the scene, show in both views, delete.
    • Points are always numbered 1, 2, 3… in order. Deleting or discarding one renumbers the rest, and pasted points are renumbered the same way. Copy the pairs again after deleting, so a saved list matches the numbers on screen.
    • Copy CSV copies the pairs. Paste points loads pairs you saved earlier, as CSV or JSON, replacing the current list. Its box always opens empty, and both views zoom to the loaded points. Clear removes all.
    • Only the coordinates and the Use flag are read from pasted data. Residuals and camera values in it are ignored and always recalculated.

    Camera

    • The panel's bar holds View from camera, Copy camera (camera only, as JSON) and Copy all (pairs and camera, as JSON, which Paste points loads back).
    • RMS error is the typical miss in pixels. σ₀ is the same corrected for the number of unknowns. Redundancy counts the extra measurements beyond the minimum.
    • ± values are one standard deviation. They shrink as you add well-spread pairs.
    • Under Also solve: Free image center solves the optical center too, for cropped photos or shift lenses. Needs 10+ pairs.
    • Lens distortion corrects lines bent by the lens. Turn it on if errors grow toward the photo edges.
    • Keep an option only if it clearly lowers the RMS error.

    View from camera

    • Moves the scene to the solved camera: same position, heading, tilt and field of view. Then it lays the photo over it.
    • If the solution is right, the buildings in the scene line up with the photo. Click the words Photo opacity to jump between 0% and 100%; keep clicking to compare. The slider sets anything in between.
    • A photo is often taken from a window or a spot the mesh encloses. Clip mesh removes the mesh within the chosen radius around the camera so it does not block the view. Click the words Clip mesh to switch the clip off and back to the last radius.
    • Click a value to type it exactly, then press Enter. A typed clip radius can go beyond the slider's 100 m.
    • Navigation is locked while the photo is shown. Exit or Esc returns you to your previous view. Explore from here instead unlocks navigation at the camera position, with the scene's default field of view.

    Photos that work

    • Ordinary camera photographs, taken from one spot through one lens: digital photos, film negatives, prints, postcards and their scans.
    • Cropped photos work with Free image center. A rotated scan is fine; the roll angle absorbs it.
    • Not suitable: orthophotos and other rectified images, maps and plans, stitched panoramas and 360° images, strong fisheye lenses, photomontages, images stretched in one direction, and photos of a print taken at an angle.
    • Features that still exist unchanged are the best points. Rebuilt or altered buildings give misleading pairs.

    Privacy

    • Your photo and point pairs stay in this browser. The app never uploads them anywhere; the solving runs locally.
    • The app does download things: the ArcGIS map library, fonts, and the tiles of the layers you view. Those servers learn which area you are looking at, not what you loaded or clicked.
    • Nothing is stored. Reloading the page resets the photo, points, added layers and all settings to their defaults.

    Coordinates and saving

    • Photo u, v are pixels from the top-left corner, v pointing down.
    • Coordinates are kept at full precision. The table shows 1 decimal for pixels and 2 for meters; copied data keeps 2 for pixels and 3 for meters (millimeters).
    • Scene X, Y are EPSG:3857, Z is meters in the mesh's height system. The camera is solved in a local metric frame and converted back.
    • Heading is clockwise from true north. Tilt follows ArcGIS: 0° looks straight down, 90° at the horizon. Pitch is tilt − 90°. Positive roll means the photo is rotated clockwise against a level camera.
    • Point pairs are not saved. Use Copy all or Copy CSV, and Paste points to continue later.
    • The example loads from the example folder next to the app: example.jpg and example.txt (points as CSV or JSON).

    Paste points

    Paste the JSON from Copy all, or CSV with the columns id,u,v,x,y,z (optional enabled), comma, semicolon or tab separated. Pasted points replace the current list. Residuals and camera values in the pasted text are ignored and recalculated.
